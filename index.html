Atomic Policy

Composing atomic manipulation skills with agentic reasoning.

Plan. Ground. Execute. Check. Repeat.

Long-horizon manipulation requires robots to translate task intent into physical interactions as the scene changes. Vision-language models (VLMs) offer semantic and spatial reasoning, while visuomotor policies provide fine-grained control. Yet ambiguous goals and dependencies between actions make their integration challenging. We introduce Atomic Policy, a framework that composes learned manipulation skills through grounded steering and execution feedback. A VLM estimates the symbolic state and goal, and a PDDL solver constructs a skill plan. An agentic harness steers each skill through language, visual pointing, or both, and verifies its outcome to guide phase transitions and replanning. A common phase-level interface supports specialized policies and generalist action models. Experiments in simulation and on a real robot show how Atomic Policy improves task following and supports recovery in complex, long-horizon manipulation.

A task is decomposed to a chain of atomic skills.

grasp handle→ open→ pick purple cube→ place in drawer→ grasp handle→ close

Each skill is reusable and has its own completion condition.
